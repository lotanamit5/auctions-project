\section{Replication of the paper's results}

Both headline numbers of the paper are computational --- Theorem~1 is the value of a linear
program solved for one grid size ($N=50$) without a dual certificate, Theorem~2 a numerical
sandwich whose grid size is not reported --- and no code is available, so before extending the
model we re-implemented every computation from the paper's formulas
(Appendix~\ref{app:repl}; software in Appendix~\ref{app:tools}). All stated numbers are
reproduced, and we record what the paper leaves unstated. For Theorem~2, an adaptive version of
the paper's grid bracket gives $0.8281462\le\alpha(\mathrm{LIP}(2))\le0.8281472$, the paper's
$[0.828,0.829]$ to seven digits. For Theorem~1, the LP value is a valid upper bound for
\emph{every} grid size and decreases with it --- $0.8412$ at $N=50$, $0.8363$ at $N=200$,
extrapolating to $\approx0.8345$ --- so the gap between LIP and the best strategy-proof mechanism
is less than half of what $0.841$ suggests.

\paragraph{Beyond the paper: $a=2$ is not the best LIP mechanism.} The paper evaluates LIP only
at $a=2$ and does not motivate the choice. Scanning $a$ with the certified bracket gives
\[
a^\star\approx2.1013,\qquad \alpha(a^\star)\approx0.82939\;>\;0.82815=\alpha(2).
\]
Figure~\ref{fig:alpha_a} explains the shape. Two restrictions of the minimisation give
rigorous upper bounds on $\alpha(a)$: along the anti-diagonal $x+y=1$ the ratio is the paper's
own intrinsic value $\theta(a)$ (Claim~8), and along the edge $y=0$ Lemma~2 gives
$Q(t)-Q(1-t)=\ln(\tfrac{x}{1-x})/a$, hence the closed form
\[
\beta(a)=\min_{b/(a+2b)\le x\le(a+b)/(a+2b)}\frac{1+\tfrac x2\big(1+\tfrac1a\ln\tfrac{x}{1-x}\big)}{1+x}.
\]
Numerically $\alpha(a)=\min\{\theta(a),\beta(a)\}$ to $10^{-6}$ on the whole scan: the edge
branch binds for $a<a^\star$, the anti-diagonal for $a>a^\star$, and the optimum is where the
two cross. Steeper prices (larger $a$) make the dictator buy a more balanced bundle, which helps
on the edge and hurts on the anti-diagonal. Together with the sharper Theorem-1 bound this
places LIP within $0.006$ of the best strategy-proof mechanism; Section~3 narrows the gap further.
