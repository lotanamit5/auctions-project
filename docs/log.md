- **Read the paper in full.** Key facts for the code: price f(t)=at+b, b=a/(e^a-1);
  spending t on an item buys Q(t)=ln(1+at/b)/a of it; Claim 5 regimes at
  b/(a+2b) and (a+b)/(a+2b); Theorem 1 LP with N=50; Theorem 2 sandwich using monotonicity

- **Observation on the chatbot draft** (commented out in `main.tex`): its "LIP under dominant
  best responses" is the same mechanism as `lip_eps_reference.py`'s eps-aware dictator: the
  reachable set of the naive rule is the whole budget frontier, so a best-responding agent
  ends up at her eps-optimal frontier point. To be verified numerically (table in the draft
  says strategic LIP min ratio 0.76515 at eps=1).
- **Observation.** For eps >= 1 the first best is always "everything to one agent" and the
  random-dictator-takes-all mechanism achieves it: competitive ratio 1. So the interesting
  regime is eps in [0, 1). Random-takes-all is LIP(a) with a -> infinity.
- **Idea for the eps upper bound.** Utility is bilinear in (z1, z2), so a lottery over
  bundles only matters through (E z1, E z2, E z1 z2). The convex hull of
  {(z1, z2, z1 z2)} over the unit square is the McCormick envelope
  {m12 >= 0, m12 >= m1 + m2 - 1, m12 <= m1, m12 <= m2}. Hence an LP over moments captures all
  randomised mechanisms exactly on a discrete type grid -> valid upper bound for the
  continuous model (type restriction only relaxes constraints).
- **Idea for LP size.** Utilities are affine in the one-dimensional type x, so full
  strategy-proofness is equivalent to adjacent-type constraints plus monotonicity of
  (m1 - m2) in x. This cuts constraints from O(N^3) to O(N^2); to be validated against the
  full LP on small N before relying on it.

### Findings so far (session 1, WP1-2)

- **Replication OK.** LP N=50 gives alpha = 0.841225 (paper: 0.841). Theorem 2 bracket for
  LIP(2): adaptive refinement certifies alpha(2) in [0.8281462, 0.8281472] in 20 ms (the
  uniform grid of the paper needs N ~ 20000 to certify the lower bound 0.828).
- **The 0.841 is an N=50 artefact.** The LP value decreases with the grid: 0.8429 (N=40),
  0.8412 (50), 0.8402 (60), 0.8388 (80), 0.8380 (100). Running larger N in the background
  (`results/data/lp_convergence_eps0.csv`). Extrapolation TBD; the true upper bound on
  strategy-proof mechanisms is strictly below 0.841.
- **a = 2 is not the best LIP parameter.** alpha(2.1) = 0.82938 > alpha(2) = 0.82815.
  Optimum a* ~ 2.1013 with alpha* ~ 0.829393.
- **Structure of alpha(a).** Two competing worst-case branches: (i) the edge y = 0 (agent 2
  all-in on item 2, agent 1 at x ~ 0.41-0.42), binding for a < a*; (ii) the anti-diagonal
  x + y = 1, binding for a > a*. Branch (ii) is exactly the profile family of Claim 8, so there
  alpha(a) = theta(a) (checked to 6 digits at a = 2.11, 2.2, 3.0). Hence
  alpha(a) = min(theta(a), beta(a)) numerically, and a* solves theta(a) = beta(a).
  Proving that the minimum over the triangle always lies on one of these two lines is open
  (TODO: try; at least the 1-D restrictions have clean formulas).
- Memory guard added to `sandwich_adaptive` after an OOM at tol = 1e-7 (cells ~ 1/tol^2).

### Session 1, eps model (WP5-6)

- **eps-aware LIP(2) reproduces the chatbot draft's 'strategic LIP' column** (0.83082, 0.84028,
  0.81002, 0.76515, 0.71528) now with certified brackets: strategic play against the naive
  rule == the eps-aware fixed-menu mechanism (same reachable set). Documented in theory/02.
- **LP upper bound rises fast with eps** (N=40: 0.843 at 0, 0.877 at 0.1, 0.953 at 0.5, 1 at
  >= 0.95 which is a grid artefact; continuous bound < 1 for eps < 1).
- **Nested (comonotone) lotteries.** Read off the LP solution (m12 = m2 in the disagreement
  region): give the dictator item j iff U < m_j with a single uniform U. Raises both agents'
  eps-terms, keeps the menu fixed (so strategy-proof), reaches ratio 1 for eps >= 1 with any a.
  At intermediate eps the dictator over-buys the cheap "pooling lottery" so the best a changes.
- **Mistake caught.** I claimed a threshold/linear-menu mechanism gives 2(1+eps)/(3+eps)
  (0.857 at eps=0.5); computing it gives 0.667: when both agents are strong for the *same*
  item, each dictator grabs it. Lesson: always compute.
- **p-norm menus** (m1/c)^p + (m2/c)^p <= 1 scanned at eps=0.5: best ~0.854 (p=1.5, c=0.6),
  far below the LP's ~0.95. Reason: a fixed-menu dictatorship cannot both pool when agents
  agree and split when they disagree; the LP-optimal mechanism conditions on both reports.
- **Certified brackets fail to close for menus with linear pieces** (dictator's choice jumps
  -> the cell bound on the other agent's utility stays loose). Report grid minima there.
- **New tool: the best swap-dictatorial mechanism on a grid is an LP** (`lp.solve_sd_lp`):
  menu items are variables, IC and welfare are linear in them. Gives the 'price of
  dictatorship' per eps, and at eps=0 tests whether any menu beats LIP(a*).
- Session killed twice by OOM from foreground experiments (MenuMechanism.choose built an
  80k x 2001 matrix; fixed by chunking). Scan scripts made resumable.

### Session 1, price of dictatorship (WP6 cont.)

- **SD-LP value at eps=0 is exactly 5/6 for every grid, certified by {0, 1/2, 1} with zero
  IC duals** -> four-profile hand proof (Theorem A, docs/theory/03). Holds for any mixture of
  two one-sided rules, lotteries included.
- **Closed forms with eps** (Theorem B): rho_A = (5+2eps)/6 (middle-type argument, small eps)
  and rho_B = (1+eps)/(1+2eps-eps^2) (three extreme profiles), exact on all grids for
  eps >= 0.3; the 2-item 'modest dictator' menu with s* = (1-eps^2)/(1+2eps-eps^2) attains
  rho_B on the continuum for eps >= 1/2.
- **Grid-optimal menus do not transfer to the continuum automatically**: N=100 menu gives
  0.8264 (< LIP), but the N=200 menu gives 0.82988 > LIP(a*) = 0.82939 — first menu mechanism
  known to beat LIP. Running N up to 800 (`experiments/sd_continuum.py`).
- **Bug/perf**: `FiniteMenuMechanism._envelope` enumerated all K^2 pairwise crossings as
  breakpoints -> cells ~ K^4 -> OOM (killed the session a third time). Replaced by a proper
  upper-envelope walk (<= K segments). Exact evaluation now ~10 ms.
- **Dead end:** direct Nelder-Mead over K=6 symmetric menu items (`experiments/menu_opt.py`)
  reaches only 0.816 (local optima; the exact ratio is piecewise and non-smooth). The SD-LP
  menus evaluated on the continuum are far better and improve with N:
  0.8197 (N=50), 0.8264 (100), 0.8299 (200), 0.8310 (300) -> approaching 5/6 from below.
- **Observation from fig. 04:** nested LIP(a*) tracks the SD-LP upper bound almost exactly for
  eps >= 0.5 (0.8568 vs 6/7 at 0.5; 0.9182 vs 45/49 at 0.8): among fixed-menu mechanisms,
  nested LIP is essentially optimal there, and the whole shortfall is the price of dictatorship.
